# Six Thinking Hats® (--join) — Example Session

## Command

```
/libertee:six-hats "Should we restructure the team into cross-functional squads?" --join red
```

## What Happens

🟦 **Blue Hat (Facilitator)** opens the session: "This is an exploratory question. You're joining as Red Hat — your real feelings about this topic, not simulated ones."

### 🔲 White Hat — Facts & Data
- Current structure: 3 functional teams (Frontend, Backend, QA) of ~6 people each
- Average feature delivery: 14 days from spec to production (involves 3 handoffs)
- Last engagement survey: 72% satisfaction, but "cross-team collaboration" scored 4.1/10
- Industry benchmark: cross-functional squads typically reduce delivery cycle time by 30-50%
- Risk: 2 of 18 team members have expressed strong preference for staying in their functional team

### 🟥 Red Hat — You

> *"Honestly? I'm frustrated. The handoffs are killing us. Every feature bounces between three teams and nobody feels ownership. But I'm also scared — the two people who don't want this are among our best engineers, and I can't afford to lose them. There's also guilt: am I pushing this because it's right, or because it's trendy? And underneath it all, a feeling I can't shake — the real problem might not be the structure at all, but that we don't talk to each other. A reorg won't fix that."*

### 🟩 Green Hat — Creativity & Alternatives
- What if we don't restructure but create temporary "mission teams" for specific features?
- What if we keep functional teams but embed a liaison in each — a "bridge" person who sits in another team one day per week?
- Wild idea: let the teams self-organize. Announce the goal (reduce handoffs) and let them propose the structure
- Reframing: Red Hat's gut feeling might be right — if the core issue is communication, maybe invest in rituals (daily cross-team sync, shared demos) before changing boxes on an org chart

### 🟨 Yellow Hat — Opportunities & Optimism
- Cross-functional squads could cut delivery time from 14 days to 7 — that's a competitive advantage
- End-to-end ownership boosts motivation: people see the impact of their work
- The two reluctant engineers might actually thrive — specialists often become valued T-shaped contributors
- This could attract talent: "full ownership, no handoffs" is a strong hiring pitch

### ⬛ Black Hat — Risks & Criticism
- Restructuring during active projects is dangerous — who owns the in-flight work during transition?
- Functional expertise may erode: the QA guild, the frontend architecture standards — who maintains those?
- The two resistant engineers are a real risk. Forcing them into squads could trigger exactly the departures Red Hat fears
- "Self-organizing" (Green Hat's wild idea) sounds great but requires psychological safety we haven't verified

### 🟦 Blue Hat — Synthesis

**Red Hat's gut was the most valuable input.** The frustration about handoffs is real and validated by data (14-day cycles, 4.1/10 collaboration score). But the fear — "the real problem might be communication, not structure" — is worth honoring.

**Recommendation:**
1. **Phase 1 (now):** Don't restructure. Instead, launch one cross-functional "mission team" as an experiment — volunteers only, 4-6 weeks, one real feature
2. **Phase 2 (after experiment):** If it works, offer squads as opt-in. Let the reluctant engineers stay functional — they'll either join when they see it working or remain as valuable specialists
3. **In parallel:** Invest in cross-team rituals regardless — shared demos, joint retros. This addresses the communication issue Green Hat and Red Hat both surfaced

**The uncomfortable truth:** The org chart change is the easy part. The hard part — building trust between teams that currently throw work over walls — takes longer and has no shortcut.
