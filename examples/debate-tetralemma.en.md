# Adversarial Debate (Tetralemma) — Example Session

## Command

```
/libertee:debate "Build vs. buy for our new CRM system" --tetralemma
```

## What Happens

⚔️ **Moderator** introduces the thesis and announces 3 rounds + judge verdict in Tetralemma mode.

### Round 1 — Opening Statements

🟢 **Pro Advocate (Build):**
- Custom CRM fits our unique sales process — no workarounds, no compromise
- Total cost of ownership over 5 years is lower: no per-seat licensing, no vendor lock-in
- We keep full control: data, integrations, feature roadmap
- Our dev team has the capacity and domain expertise

🔴 **Contra Advocate (Buy):**
- Building a CRM from scratch is reinventing the wheel — Salesforce alone has 20 years of iteration
- Time to value: buying gets us running in weeks, building takes 6-12 months minimum
- Maintenance burden: every feature we build, we maintain forever
- Our dev team should focus on our core product, not internal tooling

### Round 2 — Rebuttals

🟢 **Pro Advocate:**
- "20 years of iteration" also means 20 years of bloat — 80% of Salesforce features go unused
- Time to value is misleading: implementation, customization, and training for enterprise CRM takes months too
- Maintenance burden exists either way — vendor updates break customizations regularly

🔴 **Contra Advocate:**
- Unused features aren't a cost — unused features you built ARE
- Implementation time for buying: 3 months. Building time: 12+ months. The math is simple.
- When a vendor breaks something, they fix it. When your code breaks, your team stops working on the product

### Round 3 — Final Arguments

🟢 **Pro Advocate:**
- This isn't about saving money — it's about strategic control over a core business process
- Every company that bought and then outgrew their CRM wishes they'd built earlier
- The real question is: is our sales process a competitive advantage? If yes, don't outsource it

🔴 **Contra Advocate:**
- Strategic control is an illusion if you can't ship it — most internal tools die in development hell
- Companies that outgrew their CRM are the survivors — for every one of those, ten sank resources into builds they never finished
- The real question is: is CRM development our core competency? No, it isn't

### ⚖️ Judge's Verdict (Tetralemma)

**The One (Build is right):**
If the sales process is genuinely unique and a competitive advantage, building makes sense. The Pro side's strongest argument — strategic control over a differentiating process — holds.

**The Other (Buy is right):**
If the sales process is mostly standard with some customization needs, buying is clearly right. The Contra side's point about opportunity cost is devastating: every hour spent on CRM is an hour not spent on the product.

**Both:**
The strongest position. Buy a CRM with strong API and customization capabilities. Use it for 80% of standard workflows. Build custom modules only for the truly unique parts. This dissolves the apparent contradiction — you get speed AND control where it matters.

**Neither:**
What if the real problem isn't the tool but the process? Both sides assumed the current sales process should be preserved. Maybe the first step is to redesign the process, and the build/buy question answers itself afterward.

**None of the Above:**
The framing "build vs. buy" assumes these are the only two options. What about: partner with a CRM vendor for co-development? Or adopt an open-source CRM and contribute upstream? The question itself may be too narrow.

**Verdict:** The **Both** position best captures the truth. The build-vs-buy framing creates a false dichotomy. Buy the platform, build the differentiators.

⚔️ **Moderator's Closing:**
- Most surprising: the "Neither" position — nobody questioned whether the process itself needs work first
- Open question: which parts of the sales process are genuinely unique vs. just familiar?
