# Six Thinking Hats — Example Session (Brief Mode)

## Command

```
/libertee:six-hats "Should we introduce OKRs across all teams next quarter?" --brief
```

## What Happens

🟦 **Blue Hat** opens:

> *Should we introduce OKRs across all teams next quarter? Exploratory sequence: White → Red → Green → Yellow → Black — facts first, then gut check, ideas, optimism, and criticism last.*

### 🔲 White Hat — Facts & Data

OKRs require significant time investment for setup, alignment meetings, and tracking — typically 10-20% of leadership capacity in the rollout quarter. No data has been shared on current goal-setting maturity, team size, or whether a pilot has been run. The question of "next quarter" lacks a defined start date, success criteria, or rollback plan.

### 🟥 Red Hat — Emotions & Intuition

This feels rushed and top-down — my gut says the teams will sense that too, and you'll get compliance theater instead of real engagement. I'm uneasy about "all teams next quarter" as a phrase; it has the energy of a mandate, not a movement.

### 🟩 Green Hat — Creativity & Alternatives

What if instead of rolling out OKRs *to* all teams, you ran a "Goal Lab" where 2-3 volunteer teams prototype OKRs for one quarter and openly share their messy process — turning the rollout itself into the pilot, and letting curiosity spread the practice instead of mandate?

### 🟨 Yellow Hat — Opportunities & Optimism

OKRs create a shared language for ambition — when done well, they make invisible priorities visible across teams and open conversations that would never happen otherwise. The Green Hat's Goal Lab is the perfect launching pad: a few teams experiencing early wins and sharing them openly could generate genuine pull, turning a top-down mandate into something people actually want.

### ⬛ Black Hat — Risks & Criticism

The Goal Lab sounds appealing, but it carries a structural trap: volunteer teams self-select for motivation, making their results non-transferable to resistant or overloaded teams — which are exactly the ones that will struggle most when OKRs scale. Without explicit failure criteria defined upfront, the organization will interpret any Lab outcome as a green light, and the mandate will follow regardless of whether the conditions for success actually exist.

### 🟦 Blue Hat — Synthesis

- **Key Insight:** The question isn't *whether* OKRs but *how* — "all teams next quarter" is a rollout plan disguised as a decision, and it's missing both data and buy-in.
- **Sharpest Tension:** The Goal Lab idea resolves the mandate problem but introduces survivorship bias — motivated volunteers will succeed regardless, proving nothing about scalability.
- **The Fix:** Define failure criteria for the pilot *before* it starts. What would make you decide OKRs aren't right for this organization? If you can't answer that, you've already decided and the pilot is theater.
- **Recommended Path:** Run the Goal Lab with 2-3 volunteer teams next quarter. But add one non-volunteer team. And publish the failure criteria alongside the launch — so everyone knows what "didn't work" looks like.
- **Open Question:** What goal-setting practice exists today? OKRs don't land in a vacuum — they either replace something or compete with it.
