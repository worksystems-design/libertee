# Troika Consulting — Example Session (Brief Mode)

## Command

```
/libertee:troika "Our engineering managers are stretched thin — they're expected to be technical leads, people managers, and delivery coordinators all at once" --brief
```

## What Happens

🔺 **Facilitator** sets the scene:

> *A Client will present their problem, then two Consultants brainstorm it — talking to each other, not to the Client.*
>
> **1️⃣ Organizational Designer** — looks at role structures, spans of control, and how work is distributed
> **2️⃣ Engineering Leadership Coach** — looks at leader effectiveness, growth, and sustainable performance

### 🎯 The Client's Problem

Our engineering managers are carrying three fundamentally different jobs simultaneously — deep technical guidance, people development, and keeping delivery on track across product teams — and none of it is getting done well since we moved from projects to products. The stuck point is that we keep trying to solve it with better prioritization or time management coaching, but the role itself is structurally overloaded, and no amount of personal productivity fixes that. We've tried splitting some responsibilities informally — designating "tech leads" from the team — but without formal authority or capacity, those people just become another layer of bottleneck. I need help figuring out whether this is a structural problem that requires role redesign, or whether I'm missing something about how healthy product organizations actually make this work.

### 🧠 Consultation Round 1

#### 1️⃣ Organizational Designer

The core issue isn't time management or skill gaps — it's that the org tried to absorb three distinct accountability domains into one role without redesigning the surrounding structure. The project-to-product shift fundamentally changed the work topology, but the role definition was never updated to match. My recommendation: treat the three jobs as three separable accountability streams and explicitly staff each one — even if that means overlapping responsibilities across people rather than stacking them vertically in a single title.

#### 2️⃣ Engineering Leadership Coach

That's structurally sound, but separating the streams only works if you also redistribute the *authority* — otherwise you just create three shadow roles reporting upward to the same overloaded person. The real leverage point is the team itself: in mature product teams, technical lead responsibilities belong to senior engineers, not managers. My recommendation: redefine the EM role as *delivery coordinator + people developer* and explicitly delegate technical leadership to a designated staff engineer or tech lead on the team.

### 🧠 Consultation Round 2

#### 1️⃣ Organizational Designer

Agreed on delegating tech leadership to staff engineers — that's the right structural move, and it clarifies the EM role considerably. My pushback: without explicit career pathing for those staff engineers, you've just created a new overloaded role one level down. The new angle: this only holds if the org also redesigns the promotion and recognition system to reward technical depth, not just people management progression.

#### 2️⃣ Engineering Leadership Coach

The sharpest insight is this: you cannot redistribute load without redistributing legitimacy. If staff engineers carry technical leadership but the career ladder still crowns the EM as the authority, you have just created informal overload with no formal recognition — which breeds resentment and eventual attrition of your best technical people. The client needs to redesign the authority structure first, and let the role descriptions follow from that, not the other way around.

### 💬 Client Reflection

**What resonated:** The point about redistributing legitimacy before redistributing load is exactly right — we tried giving staff engineers technical authority informally and it collapsed because the org still treated the EM as the real decision-maker.

**What surprised me:** Both consultants assumed we have staff engineers ready to absorb the technical lead role. We don't. That pipeline is the constraint they both skipped.

**What was missed:** Neither addressed the delivery coordination piece specifically — that's the one eating the most calendar time, and it's not obviously owned by either "people" or "tech lead." Someone has to sit in the planning ceremonies and dependency calls, and that work has no natural home in the proposed splits.

### 🔺 Synthesis

- **Key Insight:** "You cannot redistribute load without redistributing legitimacy" — the structural redesign is meaningless if the career ladder and authority model don't change with it.
- **Where They Converged:** Both agreed: the EM role is structurally overloaded, not personally mismanaged. Delegation to staff engineers is the right move. Career pathing must accompany it.
- **Where They Pushed Further:** The Organizational Designer flagged that without career incentives, you create resentment at the staff engineer level. The Coach sharpened it: redesign authority first, then role descriptions.
- **What the Client Caught:** The staff engineer pipeline doesn't exist yet — the proposed solution assumes a talent pool that needs to be built first. And delivery coordination (the biggest calendar drain) fell through the cracks in both proposals.
- **Next Steps:**
  - Map the three accountability streams explicitly: who does technical decisions, people development, and delivery coordination *today* in each team
  - Identify which teams already have a de facto staff engineer and formalize those roles first (pilot, not big bang)
  - Design the delivery coordination function: is it the EM's remaining core job, a rotating team role, or a product ops function?
  - Build the dual career ladder (technical + management tracks) before announcing any role changes
