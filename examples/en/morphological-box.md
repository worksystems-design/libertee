# Morphological Box — Example Session (Brief Mode)

## Command

```
/libertee:morphological-box "How could a new internal-talk format look?" --brief
```

## What Happens

📦 **Facilitator** frames the design problem:

> *Most teams default to the same talk format. Let's see the design space and find combinations nobody pitches.*

### 🧱 Dimensions

1. **Audience commitment** — How much does the audience commit before showing up?
2. **Speaker accountability** — How much hangs on the speaker's words after the talk?
3. **Format duration** — How long does it run?

**Dependencies flagged:** None — all three independently variable.

### 🎲 Variants

#### Audience commitment
1. Drop-in (no commitment)
2. RSVP with stated question (low commitment)
3. Pre-read required + signed commitment to act on takeaways (high commitment)

#### Speaker accountability
1. None — talk evaporates after the session
2. Public follow-up post 30 days later: "what changed because of this?"
3. Audience votes: continue/abandon the speaker's proposal — binding

#### Format duration
1. 45-minute classic
2. 12-minute "ignite-style"
3. 90-minute working session, talk + workshop together

### 🔀 Combinations

**Most Surprising — Pre-read commitment / Audience-vote / 12 min**
A 12-minute pitch where attendees pre-read, vote yes/no on adopting the speaker's proposal at the end, and the vote is binding for the team. Trade-off: turns talks into commitments — kills "interesting talks" that wouldn't survive a vote.

**Most Plausible — RSVP-with-question / Public follow-up / 45 min**
Standard talk shape, but each attendee submits a question at RSVP and the speaker posts a public update 30 days later. Trade-off: keeps the comfortable format, adds soft accountability.

**Uncomfortable but Viable — Drop-in / Audience-vote / 90 min**
Anyone walks in, but the format is a 90-minute working session ending in a binding vote. Trade-off: high effort per session, low scale, but the *only* format here that combines openness with consequence.

### 🎯 The Recommendation

The **uncomfortable-but-viable** combination is the one nobody would propose unprompted, and exactly that is its value: it forces the question of whether internal talks should *do* something, not just inform. Pilot it once before deciding on a default.

**💡 The Hidden Dimension:** "Frequency" never came up — but "weekly slot for talks" is itself a design choice we didn't surface. Worth interrogating separately.
