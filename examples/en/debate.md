# Adversarial Debate — Example Session (Brief Mode)

## Command

```
/libertee:debate "Feature teams are superior to component teams for delivering customer value" --brief
```

## What Happens

⚔️ **Moderator** opens:

> *The thesis: "Feature teams are superior to component teams for delivering customer value." Three rounds, Pro vs. Contra, then a Judge rules.*

### Round 1 — Opening Statements

#### 🟢 Pro Advocate

Feature teams own the full slice of customer value from idea to delivery — no handoffs, no queues, no coordination tax. When a team can say "we ship this feature end-to-end," accountability is clear and cycle time collapses. Component teams optimize the parts; feature teams optimize the outcome.

#### 🔴 Contra Advocate

Component teams build the deep expertise and architectural integrity that feature teams cannibalize — when every team touches every layer, you get n-squared coupling, inconsistent implementations, and a platform that degrades with every "end-to-end" shortcut. The feature team's celebrated "no handoffs" is just hidden technical debt and duplicated effort, paid for later at compound interest.

### Round 2 — Rebuttals

#### 🟢 Pro Advocate

Feature teams don't eliminate expertise — they relocate it from siloed components to cross-functional collaboration, which is where customer value actually lives. The "n-squared coupling" argument describes a governance failure, not a team topology failure; strong engineering practices like inner source, API contracts, and platform teams solve this without reverting to handoff-heavy component silos. Architectural integrity maintained at the cost of delivery speed is integrity that never ships.

#### 🔴 Contra Advocate

"Relocating expertise" is a euphemism for diluting it — a team that knows everything about the customer journey knows nothing deep about the database, the network, or the compiler. And calling coupling "a governance failure" simply renames the problem without solving it; governance fails precisely *because* feature teams create the pressure that overwhelms it. The last point is a strawman: component teams that feed into a coordinated delivery pipeline ship constantly — the failure mode you're describing is a coordination problem, not an indictment of specialization.

### Round 3 — Final Arguments

#### 🟢 Pro Advocate

Feature teams win where it counts: the customer's hands. Every "deep expertise" argument describes optimization inside the organization — component teams excel at being components, not at delivering outcomes. When time-to-value is your metric, cross-functional ownership beats architectural purity every time, because shipping is the proof.

#### 🔴 Contra Advocate

Feature teams optimize for *visible* delivery while quietly accumulating architectural rot — the debt doesn't appear on the roadmap until it collapses throughput entirely. Component teams with coordination aren't slower; they're the teams still shipping reliably in year three, when feature team codebases have become untouchable. Speed to the customer means nothing if you've traded sustainable delivery for a sprint.

### ⚖️ Judge's Verdict

**Verdict: Narrow ruling for Feature Teams, with conditions.**

Pro established the decisive point — value is realized by the customer, not the component — and Contra never refuted that feature teams can match component team reliability when paired with sound governance. Contra's strongest argument (architectural rot, year-three fragility) is real but proves a failure of discipline, not of topology. The ruling stands: feature teams are superior *as a default orientation*, provided architectural stewardship is treated as a first-class concern alongside delivery.

### ⚔️ Moderator's Closing

- **Sharpest exchange:** Pro's "integrity that never ships" vs. Contra's "speed that doesn't survive year three" — both true, and the tension is the real insight.
- **What the Judge captured:** This isn't topology vs. topology — it's whether you optimize for flow or for foundations, and the answer is sequence: flow first, then invest in the foundations that sustain it.
- **Open question:** Neither side addressed the hybrid — platform teams enabling feature teams — which is where most mature organizations actually land.
