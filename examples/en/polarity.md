# Polarity Management — Example Session (Brief Mode)

## Command

```
/libertee:polarity "Standardization vs Team Autonomy" --brief
```

## What Happens

⚖️ **Facilitator** opens:

> *Standardization vs. Team Autonomy — not a problem to solve but a tension to manage. Both poles need each other.*

### 🅰️ Pole A: Standardization

**Upsides:** Consistency, predictability, and efficiency — shared processes reduce rework, lower onboarding friction, and make quality measurable across teams. Enables coordination at scale and creates a stable foundation for compliance and auditability.

**Downsides of over-focus:** Suffocates the people doing the work — assumes one-size-fits-all in contexts that are inherently different, slows response to local conditions, and breeds disengagement when teams feel their judgment is not trusted. Over-standardized systems optimize for the process rather than the outcome.

### 🅱️ Pole B: Team Autonomy

**Upsides:** Enables teams to adapt to their specific context, experiment freely, and own their outcomes — drives engagement, innovation, and faster local problem-solving. When teams have genuine agency, they optimize for results rather than compliance.

**Downsides of over-focus:** Fragments coordination, creates inconsistent customer experiences, and makes scaling knowledge nearly impossible — every team reinvents the wheel in isolation. Autonomy without the anchor of Standardization drifts into chaos, just as Standardization without Autonomy drifts into rigidity.

### ⚖️ Polarity Map

|  | **Standardization** | **Team Autonomy** |
|--|--|--|
| **Upsides (+)** | Consistency, predictability, coordination at scale, lower onboarding friction | Contextual adaptation, engagement, innovation, faster local problem-solving |
| **Downsides (-)** | One-size-fits-all, slow response, disengagement, process over outcome | Fragmented coordination, inconsistent CX, reinvented wheels, knowledge silos |

- **Virtuous Cycle:** Standardize the *interfaces* (APIs, contracts, quality gates) while leaving teams autonomous in *how* they deliver. Standards create the stable ground that makes autonomy safe; autonomy generates the local learning that makes standards smarter.
- **Vicious Cycle:** Over-standardize → teams disengage → leaders see inconsistency → standardize harder. Or: over-autonomize → chaos at the seams → leaders impose standards → teams resist → leaders give up → more chaos.
- **Warning Signs:**

| Too much Standardization | Too much Autonomy |
|--|--|
| Teams ask permission for trivial decisions | Same problem solved 5 different ways across teams |
| Process compliance becomes a performance metric | New hires can't transfer between teams |
| "That's not how we do it here" kills experiments | Customer experience varies wildly by team |

- **Action Steps:** Distinguish *what* to standardize (interfaces, quality gates, data formats) from *what* to leave autonomous (tools, practices, internal workflow). Review the boundary quarterly.
- **Where Are We Now?** The fact that this polarity was raised suggests the organization is feeling the pain of one pole. Most scaling organizations over-index on standardization first — if that's the case, the rebalancing move is: define the minimum viable standard and explicitly protect everything outside it as team territory.
