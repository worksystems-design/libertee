# Frame Check — Example Session (Brief Mode)

## Command

```
/libertee:debate "We should build our own analytics platform instead of buying one" --brief
/libertee:frame-check --brief
```

## What Happens

First, an Adversarial Debate runs on "build vs. buy" for an analytics platform. Pro argues for control, customization, and long-term cost savings. Contra argues for speed to market, proven reliability, and total cost of ownership. The Judge leans slightly toward buying, citing the engineering opportunity cost. A solid debate — clear winner, good arguments on both sides.

Then, Frame Check examines the question itself:

### 🖼️  Frame Check

#### Binary Framing

**The original frame:** "build our own ... instead of buying one"

**How it constrained:** The word "instead" forced a binary choice. Three rounds of debate explored build OR buy in depth — but "build some, buy some" (a hybrid approach) was structurally excluded. Neither advocate could argue for it because the rules required them to pick a side.

**What opens up without it:** A hybrid strategy — buy a platform for core analytics, build custom modules on top — might outperform both pure options. This is the most common real-world outcome, and the debate couldn't see it.

#### Scope Framing

**The original frame:** "our own analytics platform"

**How it constrained:** "Platform" implies a large, unified system. This framing excluded smaller options: a collection of specialized tools, a data warehouse with ad-hoc queries, or even "do we actually need analytics at all?" The scale was pre-decided.

**What opens up without it:** Maybe the real question is "what analytics capabilities do we need?" — which might lead to a mix of lightweight tools rather than a single platform.

#### Implicit Stakeholder

**The original frame:** "We should build..."

**How it constrained:** "We" implies the engineering team as the decision-maker and builder. Customer needs, data team workflows, and executive reporting requirements were discussed as secondary factors, not as the primary frame. The debate optimized for engineering trade-offs.

**What opens up without it:** Framing from the data team's perspective ("How should we enable self-service analytics?") produces a completely different solution space.

### 🔄  The Reframe

What if you had asked: **"What analytics capabilities do our teams need, and what's the fastest path to each one?"**

This reframe breaks the monolithic platform assumption and allows different answers for different capabilities — some built, some bought, some skipped entirely.
