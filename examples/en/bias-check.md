# Bias Check — Example Session (Brief Mode)

## Command

```
/libertee:six-hats "Should we adopt a four-day work week?" --brief
/libertee:bias-check --brief
```

## What Happens

First, Six Thinking Hats runs a brief exploration of the four-day work week. The synthesis leans positive — productivity research is encouraging, employee sentiment is strong, the Yellow Hat found compelling benefits, and the Black Hat's risks felt manageable. The Blue Hat recommends a pilot program.

Then, Bias Check reflects on the thinking:

### 🔍  Bias Check

#### Availability Bias

**What it is:** Overweighting vivid, easily recalled examples over base rates.

**Where it showed up:** The White Hat cited Microsoft Japan's 40% productivity boost and Iceland's national trial — both headline-grabbing successes. No failed implementations were mentioned, though they exist (e.g., companies that quietly reverted).

**What changes if you correct for it:** The evidence base shifts from "all the examples I can think of succeeded" to "the examples I've heard of were selected for newsworthiness." The pilot recommendation might need harder success criteria.

#### Anchoring

**What it is:** The first number or frame disproportionately shapes all subsequent reasoning.

**Where it showed up:** The White Hat opened with "40% productivity gain," and every subsequent hat orbited that number. The Yellow Hat built on it, the Black Hat tried to counter it but still referenced it as baseline. No hat questioned whether productivity is the right metric.

**What changes if you correct for it:** If the first data point had been "most four-day week trials show 5-15% productivity changes with high variance," the entire session would have been more cautious.

#### Group Polarization

**What it is:** Sequential perspectives amplify the direction set by earlier contributors.

**Where it showed up:** After three hats leaned positive (White's data, Red's enthusiasm, Green's creative ideas), the Yellow Hat had strong tailwind and the Black Hat was swimming upstream. The sequence created momentum that made criticism harder.

**What changes if you correct for it:** Running Black Hat earlier — or running Six Hats with a cautious sequence — might surface risks that got minimized by accumulated optimism.

### ❓  The Uncomfortable Question

If the productivity numbers are wrong — or irrelevant to your specific context — does any other argument in this analysis actually stand on its own?
