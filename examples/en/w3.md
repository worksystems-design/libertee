# W³ — What? So What? Now What? — Example Session (Brief Mode)

## Command

```
/libertee:w3 "Our first attempt at running quarterly business reviews with all product teams" --brief
```

## What Happens

🔄 **Facilitator** opens:

> *Our first attempt at running quarterly business reviews with all product teams. Three phases, strictly separated: first we observe, then we interpret, then we act.*

### 🔍 What?

- All 8 teams attended, but most sent their full team rather than just leads — the room was significantly larger than expected.
- The meeting ran over time; several teams did not get to present, or rushed through their slot in the final third.
- Content varied widely: some teams showed metrics dashboards, others brought roadmap slides, a few had no structured format and talked through status verbally.
- There was no agreed follow-up mechanism — action items were noted informally, if at all, and ownership was unclear by the end.

### 🤔 So What?

- **The format was designed for leads but pulled in everyone** — teams treated this as a cross-team visibility event, not a management review. That's a signal of hunger for alignment, but also of unclear audience definition. If that's not addressed, next QBR will be the same overcrowded, overtime mess.
- **No shared format + no follow-up mechanism = the review existed only in the room** — nothing left the meeting with teeth. The wide content variance isn't a style problem; it's evidence there's no shared model of what "good" looks like for a product team at this level. The QBR surfaced the absence of a common operating language.

### 🚀 Now What?

- **QBR owner** redesigns the format before the next cycle: define the audience (leads only, not full teams), cap each team slot at 10 minutes with a fixed template (3-5 reporting dimensions), and set a hard time box — **within 2 weeks**.
- **QBR owner + two team leads** co-create a shared reporting template this sprint — what does every product team report on? Agree on 3-5 dimensions (delivery health, strategic progress, blockers, decisions needed). This becomes the operating language.
- **QBR owner** introduces a follow-up mechanism: each team leaves with 1-3 named actions, owner, and due date in a shared doc. Status reviewed at the start of the next QBR.
- **QBR owner** sends a one-question retro pulse to the 8 team leads this week: "What would make the next QBR worth your time?" Use responses to validate the redesign.

### 🔄 Closing

The sharpest insight: the content variance across teams isn't a style problem — it's evidence that the organization has no shared operating language for what a product team reports on. The most important action: co-create the reporting template with team leads, not for them — it's the operating language that matters, not the format. Watch for this: if the next QBR still runs over time, the problem isn't the template — it's that the meeting is trying to serve two purposes (management review AND cross-team alignment) and needs to be split.
