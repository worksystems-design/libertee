# Pre-Mortem — Example Session (Brief Mode)

## Command

```
/libertee:pre-mortem "Our plan to merge three regional delivery teams into one global delivery organization over the next two quarters" --brief
```

## What Happens

💀 **Facilitator** sets the scene:

> *It's 12 months from now. The merger of three regional delivery teams into one global organization has failed spectacularly. Let's figure out why — before it happens.*

### 💀 Failure Scenarios

1. **The Invisible Hierarchy** — The merged org operates on paper as "one global team," but legacy reporting lines persist as shadow structures. People route decisions through old managers, bypassing new global leads. Root cause: existing power relationships were never dissolved.

2. **The Timezone Death Spiral** — Coordination overhead explodes as teams across three regions spend 40% of capacity in alignment meetings brutal for at least one region. Critical decisions defer to "the next sync" indefinitely. Root cause: the integration assumed global = regional at scale, with no redesign of decision rights or async workflows.

3. **The Exodus** — Highest-performing engineers and delivery leads quietly start interviewing elsewhere within the first quarter. They were hired into tight, trusted local teams. Root cause: the business case was built on headcount efficiency, not on what made regional teams effective.

4. **The Metrics Mirage** — Six months in, leadership declares success because utilization looks good on the dashboard, while delivery lead times have doubled and customer escalations are up 60%. Root cause: nobody defined what "one global org" was supposed to deliver *better* than three regional ones.

### 🔬 Reality Check

| Scenario | Verdict |
|----------|---------|
| 1. The Invisible Hierarchy | **Already doing.** Shadow structures form the moment the org chart changes; informal power follows old relationships until explicit trust is rebuilt. |
| 2. The Timezone Death Spiral | **Already doing.** Three regional offices means partial timezone overlap; 40% capacity loss is the default unless async-first is mandated from day one. |
| 3. The Exodus | **Drifting toward.** Top performers tolerate ambiguity briefly; the signal to leave is 3-6 months post-merger when clarity on ownership and career paths still hasn't arrived. |
| 4. The Metrics Mirage | **Already doing.** Utilization metrics are the wrong instrument post-merger; teams stay busy coordinating while customer-facing throughput quietly collapses. |

### 🎯 Prevention Plan

- **Top 3 Threats:** (1) Metrics Mirage — you'll think it's working while it's failing. (2) Invisible Hierarchy — the old org survives inside the new one. (3) Exodus — you have a 3-6 month window before top performers leave.
- **Preventive Actions:**
  - Replace utilization dashboards with delivery outcome metrics (lead time, customer escalation rate) *before* the merger starts — so you can see the real impact
  - Explicitly dissolve old reporting lines: reassign 1:1s, change Slack channels, move budget authority — make the old structure physically impossible to operate
  - Within the first 30 days, publish clear role definitions, career paths, and team identities in the new structure — the Exodus clock is already ticking
- **The Uncomfortable Truth:** Nobody has answered the question "what will one global org deliver *better* than three regional ones?" If the answer is "cost efficiency," the merger will optimize for cost while destroying the delivery capability that justified the headcount in the first place.
