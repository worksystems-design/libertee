# Disney Creative Strategy — Example Session

## Command

```
/libertee:disney "How could we completely rethink our onboarding?"
```

## What Happens

🎬 **Facilitator** introduces the topic and announces the three phases.

### 💭 The Dreamer's Vision

> Imagine this: new employees don't start with a laptop setup and a checklist. They start with a **personal learning journey**. From day one, they have an AI companion that answers their questions, connects them with the right people, and gives them context — not org charts, but real stories: Why did we make this decision? What was the turning point?
>
> The first week isn't "onboarding", it's an **exploration**. New colleagues solve a real problem — small, but real. By the end of the week, they've contributed something, not just consumed. They know 15 people personally, not because a calendar told them to, but because the problem led them there.
>
> After 90 days, it doesn't feel like "I'm still new" — it feels like "I belong and I've made a difference."

### 📐 The Realist's Plan

**Implementation path:**
- Week 1-2: Document current onboarding and identify the 5 most common pain points (interviews with last 10 new hires)
- Week 3-4: Build a "real problem" catalog — small, scoped tasks that teams provide
- Week 5-8: Build AI companion as prototype — start as a simple chatbot on the internal knowledge base
- Week 9-12: Pilot with 3-5 new hires, iterate

**Resources needed:**
- 1 person half-time for 3 months (project lead)
- Budget for AI tool prototype (~$5,000)
- Buy-in from 3-4 team leads for the "real problem" catalog

**First concrete step:**
Tomorrow: invite the last 5 new hires for coffee and ask: "What did you need in your first week that you didn't get?"

### 🔍 The Critic's Review

**Plan weaknesses:**
- The AI companion sounds exciting but is a technology risk. If the prototype isn't good enough, it does more harm than good
- The "real problem" approach depends on teams preparing tasks — that's extra work for people who are already at capacity
- Risk of two-tier system: pilot group gets premium onboarding, everyone else gets the old checklist

**Missing elements:**
- No fallback: what happens if the AI solution isn't ready in time?
- No measurement: how do we know after 90 days if it's better than before?
- Remote employees: the plan sounds like it works better on-site

**Strengthening suggestions:**
- Start Phase 1 without AI — validate the "real problem" concept manually first
- Define success metrics: time-to-first-contribution, satisfaction score at 30/60/90 days
- Think about the remote variant from day one

### 🎬 Facilitator — Integration

**The refined vision:**
The core image holds: onboarding as exploration, not checklist, with real contribution from day 1.

**The actionable plan:**
1. Interviews with recent hires (this week)
2. Build "real problem" catalog manually — no AI, just good tasks
3. Pilot, measure (time-to-first-contribution + satisfaction)
4. AI companion only in Phase 2, once the core concept is validated

**Risks to watch:**
- Team lead buy-in is the critical path — without the "real problem" catalog, everything falls apart
- Remote-readiness from day 1, not bolted on later

**The dream that remains:**
"After 90 days it feels like: I belong and I've made a difference." — That's the North Star metric.
